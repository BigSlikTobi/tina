# 📜 The Agentic Tax Practice (manifesto)

> 📜 **Outcome.** This is the belief we want to design against. It is proposed v0. Nothing here is built yet.
> Before we add features, we should be clear about the practice we want to make possible.

## The belief
Tax software already calculates, validates, files, imports, and tracks.
Yet the practice still runs on people carrying the invisible work between systems: finding the missing receipt, remembering what is blocked, reconciling two facts that do not agree, chasing a handover, and rebuilding context when work changes hands. Advisors are still the integration layer. They are still the workflow engine.
That is not a criticism of advisors. It is the work that requires care, judgment, and trust. But too much of their time disappears into moving information, polling status, and holding the whole practice together in their heads.
We believe a better tax practice is possible.
Not one where humans disappear. One where human judgment becomes more visible, more valuable, and more deliberately used. One where routine work moves forward until it reaches a real boundary: missing evidence, conflicting facts, professional judgment, client trust, or authority to act.
So we are not building an AI feature inside tax software. We are building an operating system for an agentic tax practice.
Every client has a durable home. Every mandate has a clear scope. Every job can be planned, worked, checked, interrupted, resumed, and proven. Context does not vanish when a person changes task, takes leave, or hands work to a colleague. It stays with the client and the work.
Agents prepare. They gather, reconcile, identify gaps, draft follow-ups, and make the next useful step visible. Advisors set intent, resolve ambiguity, apply professional judgment, own the relationship, and approve accountable outcomes.
The promise is simple: the system carries the operational weight; the advisor keeps the authority.

## What changes
Today, most tax software waits to be opened. The advisor has to know where to look, what to compare, what to ask for, and what must happen next.
In the agentic practice, work has a home and a state. It moves while it is healthy. It stops loudly when it is not. A missing document is not a hidden absence in a folder; it is a visible blocker. A contradiction is not a quiet risk in a spreadsheet; it is a question brought to the right person. A completed task is not a status someone typed in; it is an inspectable outcome with evidence behind it.
The advisor should not patrol nine modules to discover what needs attention. The practice should bring the next important exception to the advisor.
This is work by exception, not work by chasing.

## The line we will not cross
Agentic does not mean unchecked automation.
Nothing acts outside its authority. Nothing important is trusted without evidence.
An agent may prepare a review package. It may draft a client question. It may identify an unmatched transaction or an opportunity worth discussing. It may never invent evidence, hide uncertainty, contact a client, make a filing, move money, or make a legal judgment simply because it can produce convincing language.
Confidence is not proof. A clean-looking result is not an accepted outcome. A model selecting an answer does not make that answer advisor-ready.
Trust must be earned in the work itself: through clear permissions, source-linked outputs, visible checks, explicit decisions, durable history, and the ability to see what happened when it matters.
We do not need private reasoning to defend a result. We need sources, actions, checks, decisions, and stated reasons.

## The human role becomes more human
The future advisor does not spend the day copying data between tools or asking where a case stands.
They set the goal. They decide what good looks like. They resolve unclear facts and conflicting evidence. They recognize risk and opportunity in the client's situation. They make the professional calls that cannot be delegated. They own the client relationship—and they remain accountable for the outcome.
The product should make that accountability easier to exercise, not pretend to remove it.
That means the human is not the fallback when automation fails. The human is the authority the system is designed around.

## Where we begin
We begin with recurring business bookkeeping for self-employed people and SMEs—not individual income-tax filing.
This is where the pain is frequent, the evidence is tangible, and the value of better orchestration is immediate. For one bookkeeping period, the system should help prepare a typed, evidence-linked package for advisor review: reconcile what can be reconciled, surface what is missing, draft the questions that need asking, and stop where judgment or authority is required.
The first state is L2: prepare and review. Nothing irreversible. No automatic client outreach. No automatic posting, filing, payment, or final tax judgment.
Depth before breadth. One real workstream that reaches an accepted outcome is worth more than twenty shallow AI features.

## The standard we hold ourselves to
We will measure progress by accepted outcomes, not by messages sent, runs completed, tool calls made, or tokens consumed.
We will build durable client context, not temporary agent memory. We will make failure visible rather than silently guessing. We will reuse the hard-won skills, tools, checks, and patterns from each workflow. And we will expand autonomy only when measured proof says it is safe and useful to do so.
The goal is not fewer humans.
The goal is a practice where people spend their attention where it changes the outcome: on judgment, relationships, risk, and the needs of the client.
That is the agentic tax practice.
