# The Current SOTA Advisor Desk

- Current as of: 17 July 2026
- Market: German small and mid-sized tax practices
- Clients served: Self-employed people and SMEs
- Definition status: Decided for this benchmark
- Market proof: Open
- Confidence: Medium
- Full evidence: [Current SOTA benchmark](sota_benchmark.md)

> No single reviewed product delivers this complete standard today.
> It combines the strongest documented parts of the current market.
> Some parts are mature. Some remain frontier behavior that still needs proof.

## Two horizons, not one promise

This page describes the **later whole-book working-machine SOTA**. It remains the market bar for a mature Advisor Desk.

The first product promise is smaller:

```text
SE-DIFM bookkeeping mandate → evidence-linked, review-ready package → Advisor accept or return → controlled DATEV handoff
```

The first handoff is manual and human-controlled. A sanctioned DATEV API or connector is later work. The detailed contract and gates live in the [SOTA benchmark](sota_benchmark.md#current-framing--confirmed-2026-07-17).

## The short version

The later SOTA Advisor Desk is not a dashboard.

It is not a bigger task list.

It is not a tax chatbot.

It is the Advisor's daily control tower.

It sits across the tools a tax practice already uses. It keeps the important practice state current. It shows what changed. It explains what needs the Advisor's judgment and why.

It prepares the evidence. It turns the Advisor's decision into controlled progress. It keeps watching the work until it is closed.

It may also surface real client needs and advisory openings from tax changes and client evidence. That is a separate, Advisor-gated shadow lane until it proves useful.

The Advisor stays in control of professional decisions and external actions.

## Why the market needs it now

Work is spread across specialist tax tools, inboxes, portals, documents, calendars, and people.

Staff capacity is tight. Advisor time needs to move away from coordination and toward review, judgment, advice, and client decisions.

Structured e-invoices create more continuous data. Paper, PDFs, mixed formats, and late client input still remain.

AI support is arriving. Generic chat does not solve daily practice control.

Practices see client-data analysis as an opportunity for better advisory work. That opportunity still needs outcome proof.

These market facts support the product direction. They do not prove that the complete product already works or will win.

## The later whole-book loop the product must solve

```text
sync state -> detect change -> brief -> rank -> prepare -> decide -> act -> monitor -> return
```

1. Keep a current practice state across existing tools.
2. Detect deadlines, missing input, deviations, blocked work, stale state, and new client needs.
3. Explain what changed every time the Advisor returns.
4. Rank what needs judgment and show the reasons.
5. Prepare one decision packet with context, evidence, unknowns, options, and a recommendation.
6. Let the Advisor decide, correct, return, delegate, defer, or dismiss.
7. Move bounded internal work forward and stop before named professional or external gates.
8. Track waits, replies, results, retries, and next checks until closure.
9. Bring the Advisor back to the next changed state without rebuilding context.

The first wedge does not need to meet this full loop. It must prepare one supported bookkeeping period, show its evidence and blockers, earn Advisor acceptance or return, and record the controlled DATEV handoff.

## What the product must solve

| Current problem | Required product outcome |
| --- | --- |
| The Advisor rebuilds practice state from tools and memory | One current view shows the work, source, owner, state, deadline, wait, and last change |
| Important changes hide inside separate queues | Material changes become visible interventions |
| Every interruption creates a new search for context | The return brief explains what changed and what matters now |
| Priority lives in the Advisor's head | The Desk proposes an order and explains every important factor |
| Review starts with source hunting | The decision packet brings the context, evidence, unknowns, options, and decision together |
| Decisions create more coordination work | The chosen next step gets an owner, state, timing, and follow-through |
| Waiting work disappears | Every wait has a reason, owner, source, and next check |
| Delegation creates weak handovers | People and agents receive bounded work with clear review and escalation points |
| Client evidence stays trapped inside compliance work | Real client needs and advisory openings become visible to the Advisor |
| New software adds another silo | The Desk creates value across the existing stack before a full migration is needed |

## Required product capabilities

### 1. Current practice state

Join clients, work, deadlines, messages, documents, notices, people, capacity, and relevant economics.

Always show the owning source, last update, and sync health.

### 2. Change brief

Show what changed since the last visit.

Show what is fixed today, what moved, what became blocked, and what now needs attention.

### 3. Explained priority

Show why an item ranks high.

Use visible factors such as deadline, client harm, money, readiness, dependency, wait time, confidence, and capacity.

The Advisor can correct the order.

### 4. Decision packets

Put the client context, impact, sources, history, unknowns, conflicts, options, recommendation, and exact decision in one place.

Do not hide weak or conflicting evidence inside a summary.

### 5. Bounded action

Let the Desk prepare and move real work.

It can check, draft, assign, update internal state, and start an approved process.

It stops before client messages, filing, payment, signing, selling, or professional judgment unless the Advisor controls that exact step.

Broad autonomy is not required.

### 6. Wait and closure control

Track client waits, internal waits, authority waits, replies, receipts, retries, and next checks.

The work stays visible until it is closed or intentionally stopped.

### 7. Team and agent control

Show ownership, workload, handovers, review load, permissions, capability limits, and escalation points.

The Advisor should supervise judgment, not chase status.

### 8. Tax and client-value intelligence

Use named tax sources.

Mark affected-client matches as possible until checked.

Surface a real client need, the evidence, why it matters, what is unknown, and a safe next step.

Do not turn advisory into an automatic sales feed.

### 9. Trust and recovery

Show missing data, stale data, conflicts, permissions, action history, failed sync, and safe fallback.

Never show an all-clear state while a material source or decision is missing.

## What the first view must answer

The first view must answer these questions within minutes:

- What changed since I was last here?
- What needs my judgment now?
- Why is it ranked there?
- What can wait?
- What is blocked, and who owns the next move?
- Which fixed commitments shape today?
- Is there a real client need worth discussing?
- Is the data current and complete enough to trust?

This is the first-view contract.

It is not a screen design.

## The authority fence

| The Desk can move this work | The Desk must stop here |
| --- | --- |
| Read and join approved practice state | Professional or legal judgment |
| Detect changes, gaps, and blocked work | Client communication without the required approval |
| Prepare evidence and decision packets | Filing or signing without the required approval |
| Draft messages and work products | Payment or money movement |
| Assign internal work and update internal state | Automatic selling or client outreach |
| Run approved checks and workflows | Expanding its own permissions or authority |
| Monitor waits, replies, and results | Hiding uncertainty to keep work moving |

This is not weak automation.

It is real work inside a visible fence.

## Parity and the winning bar

### First-wedge acceptance

The first product proves a much smaller bar:

- one eligible SE-DIFM bookkeeping mandate;
- supported evidence, source links, clear gaps, and no false-ready result;
- Advisor accept or return;
- a recorded, manual DATEV handoff; and
- no assumption of live DATEV write access.

### Later working-machine parity

Match the important work and control of the mature DATEV stack:

- clients, jobs, tasks, deadlines, messages, documents, notices, people, checks, and history;
- source, owner, state, deadline, and last update;
- roles, rights, handovers, and four-eyes review;
- credible fit with the German production-tool world;
- clear stale, missing, blocked, and broken states.

Parity is about outcomes and control.

It is not about copying DATEV's screens or every feature.

AI is not required for parity.

### SOTA winning behavior

Win above later parity through:

- what-changed briefings;
- clear priority reasons;
- complete decision packets;
- bounded action and follow-through;
- interruption recovery;
- grounded tax-change signals;
- evidence-backed client needs and advisory openings, kept in a separate Advisor-gated shadow lane until proven.

### Market-success proof

The product still needs to prove:

- fast onboarding;
- reliable sync and clear source ownership;
- fewer missed, stale, duplicated, or wrongly cleared items;
- less time spent rebuilding context and hunting for sources;
- more Advisor time spent on judgment and advice;
- safer delegation;
- better client response;
- repeat daily use;
- economic value after integration, setup, and support cost.

## What does not qualify

- A KPI dashboard.
- A bigger inbox or task list.
- A generic chatbot.
- AI summaries that hide missing or stale information.
- An automation builder that makes the Advisor design every workflow.
- A replacement suite that creates no value before migration is complete.
- Broad autonomous client contact, filing, payment, judgment, or selling.
- A product judged mainly by clicks, logins, messages, or model usage.

## What is proven and what is not

| Status | Current read |
| --- | --- |
| Available now | German work controls, source links, rules, workflows, approvals, histories, sourced tax research, drafting, and assistive AI |
| Strong emerging behavior | Change briefs, cross-client questions, explained priority, joined decision packets, and monitored bounded agents |
| Not proven | Reliable whole-day ranking, complete affected-client matching, cross-system agent execution, safe learning from Advisor behavior, and market ROI |
| Not acceptable as a shortcut | Hidden uncertainty or autonomous consequential action |

The Desk must record accepted corrections and outcomes.

Safe automatic learning from them is not yet a proven market capability.

## How success is measured

- Time to regain control after opening or returning.
- Missed, stale, duplicated, or wrongly cleared work.
- Time spent hunting for sources.
- Context switches per resolved item.
- Work waiting without a clear owner.
- Repeated client chasing.
- Advisor time spent on judgment and advice.
- Daily repeat use.
- Economic value after integration, setup, and support cost.

No target numbers are set yet.

The five-day Advisor study and a later pilot must set them.
