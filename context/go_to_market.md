# 📣 Go-to-market (who we serve first)

> 📣 **Outcome.** We earn the right to widen the claim. We begin with the people who carry the work today: internal SE-DIFM Advisors and Tax Experts. There is no customer promise, no price, and no external launch until the work is demonstrably safer, clearer, and more valuable in their hands.

## The plan

We start inside Taxfix, with our own SE-DIFM operation—not because the external market is unimportant, but because trust has to be built where the consequences are closest and the feedback is honest. Advisors already know where context disappears, where evidence is hunted down, where client follow-up creates drag, and where a weak handover becomes someone else's hidden work.

The first offer is a design-partner experience: a daily Advisor workbench, continuity with source systems, and one deep bookkeeping-period preparation job. It is deliberately a complete experience to test, not a polished promise to sell. We want to learn whether a bounded system can help an Advisor make the day more legible, prepare eligible work into a source-linked review package, and stop visibly when it cannot safely continue.

Seven stages take us from internal discovery to open-market expansion. Each stage has a named cohort, a narrower promise than the ambition behind it, and its own proof burden:

1. **Internal discovery and replay.** Observe real work, recruit internal design partners, agree the case contract, establish the baseline, and test against synthetic cases. No live-product or outcome claim.
2. **Internal soft launch.** Make only the proven jobs available to a named internal cohort, with support, fallback, rollback, and human review in place.
3. **Seven-partner test.** Test the same bounded scope with a deliberately chosen set of tax-advisory companies. Internal learning is useful context, never a substitute for partner proof.
4. **Joint green decision.** Review the internal and partner evidence together. This is a human go, hold, or kill decision—not an automatic graduation.
5. **Selected-partner soft launch.** Offer the proven scope to named firms, with an explicit support model, incident path, contracts, and evidence-led pricing.
6. **Approved partner-network rollout.** Expand only when onboarding, quality, tenancy, support, and economics repeat reliably.
7. **Open-market expansion.** Reach a wider external segment only once demand, willingness to pay, acquisition, service, and support are as credible as the product proof.

A calendar date does not waive a gate. Momentum does not waive a gate. A convincing demo does not waive a gate. We move when the people doing the work want to continue and the quality, safety, usage, support, and value checks are green.

## The honesty rule

We test the full Kano package with design partners: the workbench and source-system continuity; a deep bookkeeping mission; visible trust and human control; one AI front door; stronger Taxfix client flow; agentic preparation; linked client communication; an evidence-backed opportunity; and an AI-assisted missing-evidence loop. The scope is intentionally ambitious because partial tests can hide where the experience breaks.

But a planned capability is never presented as a shipped one. A designed outcome is never presented as a measured one. Every claim is tied to the evidence gate it has passed, for the exact cohort, job, effect, and operating conditions that were tested.

Today, the approved runtime support is narrow. Supported work may be prepared; unsupported content must stop clearly. The Advisor retains professional judgment, client contact, acceptance, and every consequential action. Automatic client contact, selling, filing, payment, signing, and live automated external-system writes remain forbidden. DATEV and ELSTER remain systems of record; any handoff stays human-operated until separately proven and approved.

That boundary is not an apology for an unfinished product. It is the trust contract. The product should make the safe next step obvious, preserve the evidence behind it, and show its limits before a user has to discover them the hard way.

## What we need to learn

Before we can claim capacity, quality, or commercial value, we need operating proof. We will observe Advisors through normal workdays, establish a five-day baseline, and measure where effort, interruptions, evidence gaps, and handover failures actually occur. We will run a precommitted synthetic replay before relying on real-client operation. We will test whether Advisors understand the boundary, choose to use the workflow again, and can recover safely when the system reaches a blocked state.

The first case cohort remains a hypothesis: German VAT-liable freelancers and digital consultants with low document volume. It is a useful place to start because the work is bounded enough to study deeply—not a validated market segment or a pricing decision. External partner selection, partner mix, minimum evidence volume, and commercial thresholds are all later commitments, earned after internal proof rather than assumed in advance.

## Status

**Gate: go—for internal discovery recruitment only.** We can invite the right internal Advisors and Tax Experts, learn from their workflow, co-design the experience, prepare the baseline, and set up synthetic testing.

**Product gate: hold → narrow.** We do not yet have the five-day baseline, replay result, measured adoption, user confidence, safety record, partner-transfer evidence, ROI, or live controlled-use proof required to widen the offer.

**Pricing: wait for operating proof.** There is no customer price while the value, support burden, and economic shape of the work remain unproven. The first commercial question is not "what can we charge?" It is "what have we reliably made better, for whom, and under what controls?"

The next step is simple and demanding: name the sponsor and cohort, protect time for observation, fix the proof contract, and let real work—not aspiration—decide what earns the next claim.
