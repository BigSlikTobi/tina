## Two horizons, not one promise

This page describes the **later whole-book working-machine SOTA**. It remains the market bar for a mature Advisor Desk.

The first product promise is smaller:

```
SE-DIFM bookkeeping mandate → evidence-linked, review-ready package → Advisor accept or return → controlled DATEV handoff
```

The first handoff is manual and human-controlled. A sanctioned DATEV API or connector is later work.

## The short version

The later SOTA Advisor Desk is not a dashboard. It is not a bigger task list. It is not a tax chatbot.

It is the Advisor's daily control tower.

It sits across the tools a tax practice already uses. It keeps the important practice state current. It shows what changed. It explains what needs the Advisor's judgment and why.

It prepares the evidence. It turns the Advisor's decision into controlled progress. It keeps watching the work until it is closed.

It may also surface real client needs and advisory openings from tax changes and client evidence. That is a separate, Advisor-gated shadow lane until it proves useful.

The Advisor stays in control of professional decisions and external actions.

## The later whole-book loop the product must solve

```
sync state -> detect change -> brief -> rank -> prepare -> decide -> act -> monitor -> return
```

1. Keep a current practice state across existing tools.
2. Detect deadlines, missing input, deviations, blocked work, stale state, and new client needs.
3. Explain what changed every time the Advisor returns.
4. Rank what needs judgment and show the reasons.
5. Prepare one decision packet with context, evidence, unknowns, options, and a recommendation.
6. Let the Advisor decide, correct, return, delegate, defer, or dismiss.
7. Move bounded internal work forward and stop before named professional or external gates.
8. Track waits, replies, results, retries, and next checks until closure.
9. Bring the Advisor back to the next changed state without rebuilding context.

## What the product must solve

| Current problem | Required product outcome |
|---|---|
| The Advisor rebuilds practice state from tools and memory | One current view shows the work, source, owner, state, deadline, wait, and last change |
| Important changes hide inside separate queues | Material changes become visible interventions |
| Every interruption creates a new search for context | The return brief explains what changed and what matters now |
| Priority lives in the Advisor's head | The Desk proposes an order and explains every important factor |
| Review starts with source hunting | The decision packet brings the context, evidence, unknowns, options, and decision together |
| Waiting work disappears | Every wait has a reason, owner, source, and next check |
| Delegation creates weak handovers | People and agents receive bounded work with clear review and escalation points |
| Client evidence stays trapped inside compliance work | Real client needs and advisory openings become visible to the Advisor |

## Required product capabilities

**1. Current practice state.** Join clients, work, deadlines, messages, documents, notices, people, capacity, and relevant economics. Always show the owning source, last update, and sync health.

**2. Change brief.** Show what changed since the last visit. Show what is fixed today, what moved, what became blocked, and what now needs attention.

**3. Explained priority.** Show why an item ranks high. Use visible factors such as deadline, client harm, money, readiness, dependency, wait time, confidence, and capacity. The Advisor can correct the order.

**4. Decision packets.** Put the client context, impact, sources, history, unknowns, conflicts, options, recommendation, and exact decision in one place. Do not hide weak or conflicting evidence inside a summary.

**5. Bounded action.** Let the Desk prepare and move real work. It can check, draft, assign, update internal state, and start an approved process. It stops before client messages, filing, payment, signing, selling, or professional judgment unless the Advisor controls that exact step. Broad autonomy is not required.

**6. Wait and closure control.** Track client waits, internal waits, authority waits, replies, receipts, retries, and next checks. The work stays visible until it is closed or intentionally stopped.

**7. Team and agent control.** Show ownership, workload, handovers, review load, permissions, capability limits, and escalation points.

**8. Tax and client-value intelligence.** Use named tax sources. Mark affected-client matches as possible until checked. Surface a real client need, the evidence, why it matters, what is unknown, and a safe next step. Do not turn advisory into an automatic sales feed.

**9. Trust and recovery.** Show missing data, stale data, conflicts, permissions, action history, failed sync, and safe fallback. Never show an all-clear state while a material source or decision is missing.

## The authority fence

| The Desk can move this work | The Desk must stop here |
|---|---|
| Read and join approved practice state | Professional or legal judgment |
| Detect changes, gaps, and blocked work | Client communication without the required approval |
| Prepare evidence and decision packets | Filing or signing without the required approval |
| Draft messages and work products | Payment or money movement |
| Assign internal work and update internal state | Automatic selling or client outreach |
| Run approved checks and workflows | Expanding its own permissions or authority |
| Monitor waits, replies, and results | Hiding uncertainty to keep work moving |

## Parity and the winning bar

**First-wedge acceptance:** one eligible SE-DIFM bookkeeping mandate; supported evidence, source links, clear gaps, and no false-ready result; Advisor accept or return; a recorded, manual DATEV handoff; and no assumption of live DATEV write access.

**Later working-machine parity:** match the important work and control of the mature DATEV stack. Parity is about outcomes and control — not about copying DATEV's screens or every feature. AI is not required for parity.

**SOTA winning behavior:** what-changed briefings; clear priority reasons; complete decision packets; bounded action and follow-through; interruption recovery; grounded tax-change signals; evidence-backed client needs and advisory openings, kept in a separate Advisor-gated shadow lane until proven.

## What remains open

- No five-day direct Advisor observation exists yet.
- The best information order for the first view is not proven.
- DATEV and other integration feasibility is not proven.
- Buyer demand, onboarding cost, adoption, and ROI are not proven.
- The full German legal, privacy, hosting, liability, and delegation bar still needs review.

## Final position

The current product bar is clear. One connected daily home base. Current practice state. Clear changes and priorities. Complete decision context. Real work inside a visible fence. Client value without automatic selling.

The definition is decided for this benchmark. The market win is not proven. This document is not the Taxfix product vision, roadmap, MVP, or current build.
