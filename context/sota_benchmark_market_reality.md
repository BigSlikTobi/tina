# 🔭 SOTA benchmark (market reality)

> 🔭 **Outcome.** We looked hard at the market before deciding anything. SOTA is analysis, not the product decision. It grounds every choice that follows and it keeps us honest about how much harder the real job is than a neat dashboard makes it look.

## What we found
German tax practices are not waiting for another feature. They are carrying a growing volume of deadlines, client follow-ups, documents, approvals, and exceptions through a fragmented working day, often with too few people, uneven client input, and little time to rebuild context after every interruption.
The pressure is structural, not cosmetic. Staffing gaps remain material, the profession is aging, and satisfaction has fallen. At the same time, the work is becoming more digital without becoming simpler: intake still arrives through email, scans, portals, PDFs, phone photos, and paper; structured e-invoicing adds a continuous, machine-readable stream that must coexist with all of it. The result is not a clean digital back office. It is a practice that has to keep moving while repeatedly asking: *What changed? What is blocked? What needs a human judgment now?*
DATEV owns the ground. Its desktop stack is deep, widely embedded, and built around the real mechanics of German practice work: orders, deadlines, tasks, checklists, documents, handovers, rights, and four-eyes controls. It is the system of record, not a competitor we can wish away. But depth does not automatically create a single, calm, trusted view of the day. Orientation still has to be reconstructed across specialist tools, channels, and people.
The market frontier is therefore split:
- **DATEV** sets the German execution and control bar. It proves the work is serious, regulated, and full of operational detail.
- **milia** is the closest modern German whole-day reference: matters, tasks, deadlines, owners, approvals, workflow, and history in one operating surface. Its AI is currently mostly assistive; multi-step agents are still ahead of broad availability.
- **Karbon** shows the adjacent interaction ceiling. Its daily-work spine and Kai briefing point to a machine that can surface blocked and overdue work, answer live firm questions, invoke help, and put an Advisor in an approve, edit, or reject loop. It is early, global, and not DATEV-native — but it makes the direction visible.
- **Taxy.io** shows what grounded tax assistance can look like: answers with named sources and links, rather than a confident black box. CoCounsel and Intuit reinforce parts of this pattern through cited research, review support, anomaly views, and portfolio context.
No reviewed product proves the whole combination: German tax depth, DATEV fit, reliable evidence, broad availability, and safe agentic behavior across the working day. That absence matters. It means we should neither lower the bar to a nicer task list nor pretend that the market has already solved trusted autonomy.
The BStBK boundary is equally clear. AI can prepare, extract, research, and check. It can be valuable precisely because it reduces the mechanical burden around professional judgment. But it can also be false or incomplete, and the Advisor remains responsible. The product standard is therefore not an agent that disappears into the background. It is a system that makes progress visible, evidence inspectable, uncertainty explicit, and the human decision unmistakably theirs.

## The one decision that shaped everything
We split the market into **two horizons** and refused to blend them. This is the central discipline in the work.
- **First wedge:** prepare one SE-DIFM bookkeeping period into an evidence-linked, review-ready package, then make a controlled DATEV handoff.
- **Later:** a whole-book working machine across the practice — one that maintains current state, explains priority, recovers context after interruptions, prepares decisions, and moves bounded work forward under human control.
The later horizon is deliberately ambitious. It is the market SOTA we should learn from: not a dashboard, not a chat window, and not a suite replacement, but an Advisor operating and decision layer across the systems that already hold the truth. A strong version would notice changes and exceptions, rank what deserves attention, show the sources and the reasoning, prepare the next best action, and learn from explicit corrections. It would make the practice feel less like a set of queues and more like a working system.
But it is not the first claim. The first wedge earns its right to exist through a smaller, harder proof: can we turn one bookkeeping mandate into a package an Advisor can actually review with confidence? Can every important conclusion lead back to evidence? Are blockers and unknowns visible rather than hidden? Can the Advisor accept or return the work without losing control? And can the package cross into DATEV without pretending that a handoff is an integration?
That boundary protects us from a familiar failure mode: borrowing the language of an entire operating system to describe an early workflow. The first product does not need to be the whole practice command center. It needs to be so trustworthy on a bounded, high-value piece of work that expanding the scope becomes credible.

## Boundaries we fixed early
Three boundaries keep the benchmark practical.

**1. DATEV remains authoritative.** DATEV-owned production data stays in DATEV. Taxfix owns its own case, task, run, evidence, review, and handoff records. We begin with a manual, human-controlled handoff because it lets us test the review-ready package before we make claims about live access, write permissions, reconciliation, or integration reliability. Any later connector needs a sanctioned path, named authority, tenant approval, clear source mapping, the minimum necessary data flow, and visible human gates.

**2. Opportunity detection is a shadow signal, not a sales machine.** There may be value in noticing an evidence-backed client need or advisory opportunity. But this is separate from normal bookkeeping parity and separate from automatic outreach. The right early form is a candidate signal with confidence, sources, and unknowns — then an Advisor can save it, dismiss it, or decide to start a conversation. It earns a larger role only through observed precision and Advisor trust.

**3. Vendor signals are not proof.** GFR.ai, Klardaten, and similar products are useful indications of where the market may be moving. They do not prove access, custody, reliability, permitted integration, customer value, or product support for Taxfix. We will not build certainty on another vendor's marketing claim.

## What this raises the bar to
For the first wedge, parity is not copying DATEV screens and it is not adding AI for its own sake. It means matching the parts of German work that create trust: source fidelity, clear ownership, rights and handovers, visible checks, safe progress, and a clean return path when something is wrong.
For the later horizon, the bar is higher still: maintain live practice state → detect changes and exceptions → explain and rank interventions → prepare a sourced decision packet → let the Advisor decide → execute only bounded work → monitor and learn from explicit corrections.
That is the emotional core of the benchmark. The Advisor should not have to carry the entire practice state in their head. They should be able to return from an interruption and know what matters, why it matters, what is known, what is missing, and what they can safely do next. The machine's role is not to replace accountability. It is to make accountable work feel possible again.

## Status
**Gate: not-decided.** This is input. It sets the bar; it does not pick the product.
The current Taxfix build did not set the bar either. The market did. Real Advisor work did. The next evidence has to come from direct observation and a bounded proof: five normal working days, one review-ready bookkeeping package, one controlled handoff, and an honest record of where people still have to reconstruct the story themselves.
